A Monument That Changed the World

Built by the Portuguese in 1482 as São Jorge da Mina (Saint George of the Mine), Elmina Castle is the oldest European building in sub-Saharan Africa. It witnessed centuries of the transatlantic slave trade before passing to Dutch and then British hands — and finally to independent Ghana in 1957.

Today it stands as a symbol of remembrance, resilience, and cultural pride — drawing visitors from every corner of the globe.

Important Facts To Know

Portuguese Origins

Constructed in 1482 by the Portuguese as a trading post for gold and ivory.

The Door of No Return

Enslaved Africans passed through this gate onto ships headed for the Americas — never to return.

Three Colonial Powers

Portugal, the Netherlands, and Britain each controlled the castle across its history.

UNESCO Since 1979

Recognised as a World Heritage Site alongside the other castles and forts along Ghana's coast.

Living Museum

The castle now serves as an educational site, drawing researchers, diaspora visitors and students.

Elmina Town

The surrounding town is one of Ghana's oldest fishing communities — a living heritage in itself.
